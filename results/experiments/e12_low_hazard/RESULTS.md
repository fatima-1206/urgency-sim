## Verdict: the equal-airtime result extends to 45 alarms per mission

This experiment existed to close a structural gap. Every other comparison in the programme
is matched only at roughly 9--11 alarms per mission, because the threshold baseline
saturates there and cannot be driven higher. At a lower hazard level more of the field
sits above the threshold, so the baseline has more crossings to fire on and the budget can
be matched at a genuinely high rate.

### P1 — the budget becomes reachable. HELD.

| hazard | baseline @ 45 achieved | matched? |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | **44.98** | yes |
| 0.15 | **45.40** | yes |
| 0.20 | 36.88 | saturated, flagged |
| 0.25 | 22.06 | saturated, flagged |

The proposed method hits every budget at every level (44.80, 44.89, 44.91, 45.06). At
hazard 0.10 and 0.15 **both methods spend the same airtime at 45 alarms per mission** —
the first such comparison anywhere in this project.

### P2 — the margin stays negative. HELD.

| budget | matched cells | mean margin | baseline better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 8 | −0.0395 | 8/8 |
| 22 | 10 | −0.0270 | 9/10 |
| 45 | 7 | −0.0235 | 6/7 |
| **all** | **25** | **−0.0300** | **23/25** |

The prediction was the unfavourable one and it held. Giving the baseline a budget it can
actually spend does not reverse the result; the proposed method still loses at equal
airtime, now demonstrated up to 45 alarms rather than only at 11.

### P3 — the latency penalty. MIXED.

The prediction was that the matched-airtime latency penalty would continue falling as the
hazard fell, reaching ≤ 0 at hazard 0.15 or below. Measured, in ticks:

| hazard | 0.10 | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| matched latency | +0.3 | +0.1 | +0.6 | −0.6 |

The **direction** held and more strongly than predicted: against +17 to +44 ticks at
hazard 0.50 (E7), the penalty has vanished across this entire range. But it is **flat near
zero rather than monotone**, and at hazard 0.15 it is +0.1, not ≤ 0. The specific numeric
form of the prediction is therefore not met, and it is scored mixed rather than held.

### The controlled demonstration

The clearest evidence in the programme is inside this one experiment. Every row below is
the **same method at the same requested budget of 45**. Only the hazard level changes, and
with it whether the baseline can spend that budget.

| hazard | baseline fired | out-spend ratio | on-time margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | 36.9 – 49.4 | 0.92 – 1.07× | −0.051 to **+0.003** |
| 0.15 | 37.6 – 50.7 | 0.88 – 1.05× | −0.045 to −0.020 |
| 0.20 | 29.8 – 40.9 | 1.12 – 1.41× | −0.032 to +0.022 |
| 0.25 | 18.4 – 24.9 | 1.86 – 2.23× | **+0.034 to +0.104** |

As the baseline saturates, the out-spend ratio climbs and the margin flips sign. **Nothing
about the proposed method changed between these rows.** Where the programme's other
experiments establish this relationship by correlation across 332 cells, this establishes
it by control within one.

### What this closes, and what it adds

It **closes** the limitation recorded in `SUMMARY.md`: that no matched comparison existed
at a high alarm rate and none was possible against this baseline. One is now possible, and
was run.

It **adds** one qualification. Pooled over all experiments, the deficit narrows as the
budget rises — −0.0718 at budget 5, −0.0562 at 11, −0.0270 at 22, −0.0235 at 45 — and two
of 79 matched cells reach a statistical tie (h0.10 predictable/sparse at budget 45,
+0.003 ± 0.033; h0.20 unpredictable/sparse at budget 22, +0.003 ± 0.041). Two cells out of
79 is not parity, and the deficit does not reverse at any budget tested. The honest
statement is that it narrows roughly threefold and stops short of zero.
