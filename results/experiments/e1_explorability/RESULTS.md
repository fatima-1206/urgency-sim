## Read this before reading the tables above

Two faults in the experiment's design were found after the run. Both are recorded in
`PROTOCOL_EXPERIMENTS.md` §8. Neither changes a number; both change what the numbers
are allowed to mean.

### 1. The alarm budget is not matched at 8 of the 10 cells

The whole comparison rests on giving both methods the same airtime. Here is what they
actually spent:

| level | budget | proposed fired | threshold fired | ratio | on-time diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| t200 | 11 | 12.1 | 2.5 | 4.75× | +0.119 |
| t200 | 45 | 36.6 | 2.5 | **14.4×** | +0.325 |
| t400 | 11 | 10.5 | 4.3 | 2.44× | +0.041 |
| t400 | 45 | 43.1 | 4.3 | **10.0×** | +0.277 |
| t800 | 11 | 11.1 | 10.1 | **1.10×** | −0.056 |
| t800 | 45 | 49.6 | 10.1 | 4.91× | +0.117 |
| t1600 | 11 | 10.2 | 10.9 | **0.94×** | −0.070 |
| t1600 | 45 | 48.3 | 21.1 | 2.29× | +0.010 |
| n60 | 11 | 11.9 | 4.5 | 2.61× | +0.011 |
| n60 | 45 | 48.0 | 4.5 | **10.6×** | +0.029 |

The threshold baseline **saturates**. Its alarm count is bounded by how many observed
cells exceed the hazard line, and no trigger setting raises that — at t200 it manages
2.5 alarms however far its trigger is driven toward zero. So the large positive
differences at budget 45 are not evidence that urgency ranking beats a hazard
threshold. They are evidence that **the proposed method can use airtime the baseline
cannot**, which is a different and much weaker claim.

Only two cells are genuinely matched, t800 @ 11 (1.10×) and t1600 @ 11 (0.94×), and the
proposed method **loses both** (−0.056, −0.070). That agrees with Phase 3.

So the pre-registered prediction — *the advantage grows as explorability falls* — cannot
be scored from this experiment. At the low-explorability levels where the advantage
looks largest, the baseline could not be calibrated to the budget, so there is no
matched comparison to read the advantage off. **E1 is reported as inconclusive on its
primary prediction.** The baseline-saturation finding is reported in its place, as an
exploratory result.

### 2. Level t200's scenario factor is degenerate

`wind_shift_t = 347` and `new_source_t = 427`. A 200-tick mission ends before either
happens, so "predictable" and "unpredictable" are the same simulation at t200. The two
scenario rows are identical by construction — visible in the tables above, where every
t200 pair matches to the last decimal. **They are one result, not two**, and t200
contributes half the cells it appears to.

### What E1 does establish

Explorability alone does not drive the effect. Compare two levels at nearly the same
explorability but different mission length:

| level | explorability | mission | on-time diff @ 45, predictable/dense |
|---|---|---|---|
| t400 | 0.25 | 400 ticks on 1,600 cells | +0.295 |
| n60 | 0.22 | 800 ticks on 3,600 cells | +0.060 |

Nearly the same explorability, a five-fold difference in measured effect. Whatever is
driving the short-mission result, **it is mission length, not the ratio of mission to
domain size** — which is the opposite of the premise E1 was built to test. The regime
hypothesis in `PROTOCOL_EXPERIMENTS.md` §1 is not supported by its own headline
experiment.

A plausible mechanism, offered as a hypothesis and not tested here: the differences
track how badly the baseline saturates (14.4× at t200, 10.0× at t400, 10.6× at n60 —
and n60 is the outlier that breaks the pattern, so even this is shaky). Testing it
properly needs a baseline that can be driven to an arbitrary alarm rate, which the
hazard threshold is not.
